Marina Seaside

A table above the shoreline on the Kenyan coast, where the kitchen follows the boats and every plate arrives with a view of the water.

We sit just back from the beach at Mtwapa, raised enough to see the whole bay — the sand, the moored dhows and the yachts. Mornings are for slow coffee and fruit; evenings belong to charcoal, lanterns and the horizon.

The Restaurant

An open-air room
with the ocean for a wall

Thatched makuti above, warm teak underfoot and woven lanterns that glow as the sun drops. The terrace looks straight out over the beach and the anchorage, so lunch stretches into sundowners without anyone noticing.

The kitchen works with whatever the dhows bring in that morning — snapper, prawns, crab, octopus — finished over charcoal with coconut, tamarind and lime. Swahili roots, an easy hand, nothing overworked.
